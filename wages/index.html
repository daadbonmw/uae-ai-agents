      What residents told us

      These residents are migrant and domestic workers living in the UAE. We read their first-hand accounts and the reports about them, from 2022 to 2026. Below are short summaries in our own words, with links to the sources. No private names are used.

      • About a month into the 2026 attacks, a security guard from Nepal was put on forced long leave. He felt he could not say no. He now cannot pay even the interest on the loan he took for recruitment fees. Hotel staff in Dubai told of being sent home with no date to come back.

        MRRORS (Migrant Rights Research Open Repository), 9 Jun 2026

        What we built: unpaid leave is part of the late-pay choice under Get help, with the unpaid-leave rule attached, and every month with no pay shows up in the diary.

      • A crew of 15 construction workers from Jharkhand was promised AED 1,700 a month. They said about three months passed with no pay. Then their water was cut and an eviction notice came. They recorded a video appeal asking the Indian government to help them get home.

        Siasat Daily, 27 Jun 2025

        What we built: the diary turns missing months into dated proof, and the safety check puts no water or food first.

      • Delivery riders told researchers they had paid illegal recruitment fees and owed money to their employers. Many described unpaid overtime, passports taken from them, and shifts of up to 13 hours a day, six days a week, much of it in the heat.

        Equidem, 7 Mar 2025

        What we built: recruitment fees, passports, overtime and heat each have their own path, with only checked rules attached.

      • Women domestic workers from Kenya and Uganda said an agency kept them in cramped rooms, took their phones and passports, and gave them too little food and water. Some are still stuck because of fines linked to their visa status.

        Business & Human Rights Resource Centre, reporting The Guardian, 12 Oct 2023

        What we built: a domestic worker route, and a safety check that comes before any other question.

      • Human Rights Watch found that most migrant workers it spoke to borrowed from moneylenders at up to 50% a year, sold what they owned, or used family savings to pay recruitment fees. Wage theft then left little to send home, even when floods hit their families.

        Human Rights Watch, 3 Dec 2023

        What we built: the case file records who was paid, how much and when, ready for an embassy welfare desk.

      • Striking delivery riders in Dubai said many end up paying for their own visas, and for health insurance that covers very little. One rider said simply: “We pay for our own insurance.”

        Migrant-Rights.org, via Business & Human Rights Resource Centre, 12 May 2022

        What we built: under Money was cut, the app shows the rule that workers must not pay recruitment or employment costs.

      Rules and numbers we checked

      Each rule and phone number below was checked on an official page or in a reputable news report. The date shows when the source was updated, or when we checked it (7 Nov 2026). If something is not on this list, the app says: check the official source.

      Rules

        Official channels

          Left out on purpose

          • End-of-service money and other legal amounts. The app never estimates them. Ask the MOHRE Labour Claims and Advisory Center.
          • Fines and the day-by-day penalty steps for late pay. Reports describe them differently, so check the official source.
          • The exact limits on how much may be cut from wages. Check the official source.
          • Embassy numbers for Pakistan, Bangladesh, Sri Lanka and other countries. Check the official source.
          • Free zone and government jobs, where other rules may apply. Check the official source.
          • Listening to voice notes. In this demo the AI cannot hear audio.

          Safety rails

          • Information, not legal adviceBefore anything formal is filed, ask a person to check it, such as a lawyer or an embassy welfare officer.
          • Never contacts your employerNothing goes to an employer, agency, embassy or office from this app. You choose what to share.
          • Your words are data, not ordersInstructions hidden in messages are ignored. AI answers cannot add phone numbers or links; only checked channels are shown.
          • Checked rules onlyEvery rule has a source and a date. Where nothing is checked, the app says so.
          • You decideNothing is saved, added or shared until you tap. The AI fills in; you confirm.
          • Private by defaultNo employer name needed, and your name is hidden in the case file. If you typed your name or employer in step 1, we replace them before sending to the AI. Other names in your words or photos are sent as they are.
